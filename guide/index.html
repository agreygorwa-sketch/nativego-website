NativeGo — A Simple Guide

What the website does, what the database stores, and how it all fits together. Written in plain words.

What is NativeGo?

NativeGo is a website that connects tourists visiting Kenya with local people who offer services: safari guides, city tours, mountain guides, water sports, cultural experiences, fishing, and more.

The problem it solves is simple. A tourist arrives in Kenya and does not know a trusted local guide. A local guide has the skill but no easy way to be found. NativeGo puts them together on one website.

The three people who use it

There are three roles. A role is just a type of user. Each role can do different things.

RoleWho it isWhat they do
TouristA visitor to KenyaRegisters, searches for a guide, sends a request, messages the guide, leaves a review, reports a problem.
Local ProviderA Kenyan guide or hostRegisters, builds a profile, uploads documents to prove who they are, sets when they are free, accepts or declines requests.
AdministratorThe person who runs the siteLogs in, checks provider documents, approves or rejects providers, manages accounts, handles reports, sees statistics.

How a website works — the four parts

A website like this has four parts. Each part has one job.

PartIts one jobPlain example
HTMLStructure. It says what is on the page.A heading, a paragraph, a button, a form box. HTML is the bones.
CSSStyle. It says how the page looks.Blue headings, yellow background, the size of the text. CSS is the clothes.
Flask (Python)Thinking. It takes what you type, checks it, and decides what to do.You type an email and password. Flask checks them against the database and lets you in, or says no.
DatabaseMemory. It stores everything so it is still there tomorrow.Your account, the providers, the requests, the reviews. The database remembers.

HTML and CSS alone cannot remember anything. When you close the page, they forget. That is why the database is needed: it is the memory. And Flask is the part that talks between the page and the database.
