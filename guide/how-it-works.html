NativeGo — How It Fits Together

One full journey, step by step, showing which requirement it is and which database table it uses.

The journey: a tourist books a safari guide

Follow one tourist, Wanjiku, from start to finish.

StepWhat happensRequirementDatabase table
1Wanjiku opens the site and sees the home page, the guides, and the experiences.—— (reading only)
2Wanjiku registers: name, email, password.FR-01users (one new row, role = tourist)
3Wanjiku logs in with email and password.FR-02users (read the row)
4Wanjiku searches: location "Maasai Mara", service "Safari Guide".FR-03providers, reviews (for the rating)
5Wanjiku opens a provider profile and reads the reviews.FR-04providers, reviews
6Wanjiku sends a request: date, 2 guests, meeting point, notes.FR-05requests (one new row, status = pending)
7The provider, David, sees the request and accepts it.FR-13requests (status → accepted)
8Wanjiku and David exchange messages about the meeting point.FR-06messages (one row per message)
9After the safari, Wanjiku leaves a 5-star review.FR-07reviews (one new row); providers (average_rating updated)
10The administrator sees the numbers: how many users, requests, reviews.FR-19users, requests, reviews

The request moves through states: pending → accepted or declined → completed → reviewed. Only once the booking is marked completed does the review path unlock.

The provider side, in short

David registers as a provider (FR-09), builds his profile (FR-10), uploads his ID and certificate (FR-11), sets his free days (FR-12). His profile is hidden until the administrator approves him (FR-16) — then tourists can find him.

The Flask system does all 19 requirements and runs live at https://nativego-flask.vercel.app. Live M-Pesa payment is not built yet — the payments table is ready, but no real money moves.

Source: Tables 4.1 and 4.3 in the project document (Chapters 1–4), and the built Flask system.
