The journey: a tourist books a safari guide

Follow one tourist, Wanjiku, from start to finish. Each step names the requirement, the page, and the table.

StepWhat happensRequirementWhat does the workDatabase table
1Wanjiku opens the site and sees the home page, the guides, and the experiences.—HTML shows the page. CSS makes it blue and yellow.— (reading only)
2Wanjiku registers: name, email, password.FR-01Flask checks the input, hashes the password, creates the account.users (one new row, role = tourist)
3Wanjiku logs in with email and password.FR-02Flask hashes what she typed, compares it with the stored hash, opens a session.users (read the row)
4Wanjiku searches: location "Maasai Mara", service "Safari Guide".FR-03Flask queries only verified providers, ranks by closeness.providers, reviews (for the rating)
5Wanjiku opens a provider profile and reads the reviews.FR-04Flask fetches the profile, services, and reviews.providers, reviews
6Wanjiku sends a request: date, 2 guests, meeting point, notes.FR-05Flask creates a pending request and tells the provider.requests (one new row, status = pending)
7The provider, David, sees the request and accepts it.FR-13Flask updates the status and tells Wanjiku.requests (status → accepted)
8Wanjiku and David exchange messages about the meeting point.FR-06Flask stores each message linked to the request.messages (one row per message)
9After the safari, Wanjiku leaves a 5-star review.FR-07Flask checks the service is finished, stores the review, updates the average rating.reviews (one new row); providers (average_rating updated)
10The administrator sees the numbers: how many users, requests, reviews.FR-19Flask counts the rows and shows a dashboard.users, requests, reviews

The provider side, in short

David registers as a provider (FR-09) — a row in users and a row in providers. His profile is hidden until the administrator approves him. He builds his profile (FR-10) — bio, languages, rates. He uploads his ID and certificate (FR-11) — files checked and stored, marked pending. He sets his free days (FR-12) — stored in providers, so he shows in search. The administrator checks his documents and approves him (FR-16) — verified becomes TRUE, and tourists can now find him.

Which part does what — the simple rule

When you see or do thisThe part that does it
Words, headings, buttons, and form boxes on the pageHTML
Colours, fonts, spacing, layoutCSS
Checking a password, saving a request, searching, countingFlask (Python)
Remembering it all after you close the pageDatabase (nativego_db)

Why the database is needed

HTML and CSS are files. They show the same page to everyone and they remember nothing. If Wanjiku registers and there is no database, her account is gone the moment she closes the page. The database is the memory: it stores the account, the request, the messages, and the review, so they are still there tomorrow, for the right people only.

What is already built, and what is not

Built: the Flask system does all 19 requirements (FR-01 to FR-19). It has 121 tests that pass. It runs on a laptop at localhost:5050. The 9-page static website is the front of the shop. Not built yet: live hosting (so others can open it from their own phones), and live M-Pesa payment (the payments table is ready, but no real money moves yet — that is a future step, and it is marked as prospective in the project document).
